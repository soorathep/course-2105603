Lab P2 · Equations of state against reference data

How wrong is an equation of state, and where?

Eight isotherms of four fluids from the NIST reference equations of state. The lab computes Z at every tabulated pressure from the ideal gas, the truncated virial equation with the Pitzer correlation for B, and the van der Waals, Redlich–Kwong, Soave–Redlich–Kwong and Peng–Robinson cubics, then reports the errors in the gas-like and the dense region.

Choose an isothermAll reference values come from the NIST reference equations of state.

Data: NIST Chemistry WebBook, SRD 69 (Lemmon, McLinden and Friend), from the reference equations of state of Setzmann and Wagner (methane), Span and Wagner (CO₂), Lemmon, McLinden and Wagner (propane) and IAPWS-95 (water). These are computed values with uncertainties in density of about 0.03–0.1% in most of this range, far below the errors of the models tested here. The acentric factor ω is computed from the NIST vapor pressure at T = 0.7 Tc.

Data and method

Each isotherm carries 200–400 reference points from 0.02 Pc (or 0.25–1 bar) to 3 Pc (or 200–250 bar). Errors are 100(Zmodel − Zref)/Zref, averaged as absolute values below and above the critical density ρc. The virial equation is evaluated only for gas points with ρ ≤ ρc. SRK uses m = 0.480 + 1.574ω − 0.176ω²; PR uses m = 0.37464 + 1.54226ω − 0.26992ω²; α = [1 + m(1 − Tr1/2)]². R = 8.31446 J/(mol K).

    4. Check your understanding

    Write first, then compare

    Use Steps 1–3 to answer each question in your own words. Then open the explanation and compare. Your answers are saved with your study.

    A. Why do the cubics fail more for the liquid than for the gas?

    Calculate CO₂ at 280 K, propane at 300 K and water at 500 K. Compare the errors below and above ρc.

    Compare with an explanation

    In a gas the attraction term is a small correction and any reasonable a and b give Z to 1–3%. A liquid volume is set by how b and a(T) balance near the close-packed limit, and each cubic has one fixed critical compressibility (PR 0.307, SRK 0.333) while real fluids have 0.23–0.29. SRK and PR tune α(T) to vapor pressures, not to densities. PR happens to give liquid CO₂ at 280 K to 1.6%, but propane at 300 K is off by 5% and water at 500 K by 23%: a hydrogen-bonded liquid is outside what two constants and ω describe.

    B. Why does the truncated virial equation fail for a dense gas when its B is accurate?

    Calculate methane at 300 K and at Tr = 1.05. Compare the virial error with B from the data (graduate A) or with the Pitzer value in the table.

    Compare with an explanation

    Z = 1 + BP/RT keeps only the first density term. The Pitzer B for methane at 300 K is −41.3 cm³/mol against −42.2 from the data, 2% apart, yet the gas-region error is 5.7% because at 100–250 bar the C/V² term and higher terms matter. At Tr = 1.05 the gas is closer to its critical density and the error doubles. Use the truncated form only where BP/RT is small, roughly Vr > 2.

    C. What is the middle root?

    Choose CO₂ at 280 K, Peng–Robinson, and set the inspection pressure to 30 bar. Read the three roots and the sign of (∂P/∂V)T.

    Compare with an explanation

    At 30 bar PR gives Z = 0.0689, 0.128 and 0.769. The largest is the vapor (reference 0.779 at this pressure), the smallest the liquid. The middle root has (∂P/∂V)T > 0, so κT < 0: compressing it would lower its pressure, which violates the stability condition of Session P1. At 30 bar, below the vapor pressure of 41.6 bar, the vapor root also has the lower GR/RT, so it is the stable phase.

    4. Investigate what an equation of state can support

    Graduate work

    Calculate an isotherm in Step 1 before running an investigation.

    What these results can and cannot show

    • The reference values are computed from multiparameter equations fitted to measurements. Their uncertainty (about 0.1% in density here) is small against the errors tested, so they serve as data.
    • The cubics use only Tc, Pc and ω. A good result on one isotherm does not validate them on another; a bad result on water says nothing about methane.
    • Z errors are not the whole story: fugacity and enthalpy depend on integrals of Z and can be better or worse (Sessions P3 and P4).
