Lab P3 · Residual properties and process energy

From Z to H, S and G: how much energy does the real fluid need?

An enthalpy or entropy change of a real fluid is an ideal-gas change plus the difference of two residual properties. The lab computes HR, SR and GR from the truncated virial equation and four cubic equations, compares them with the NIST reference, and uses them to find the outlet of two valves and two compressors.

Data and method

Reference residuals: HR = H(T, P) − H(T, 0.001 bar) and SR = S(T, P) − S(T, 0.001 bar) + R ln(P/0.001 bar), from NIST isobars in steps of 0.5 K. The cubics choose the root of lower Gibbs energy except at the tabulated state, where the reference phase is used. The virial equation is Z = 1 + BP/RT with the Pitzer B(T) and dB/dT. Process outlets are found by bisection on H (valve) or S (compressor). The methane and CO₂ Shomate equations are fitted from 298 K; below that they are extrapolated.

    4. Check your understanding

    Write first, then compare

    Use Steps 1–3 to answer each question in your own words. Then open the explanation and compare. Your answers are saved with your study.

    A. Why does the ideal gas overestimate the work of the CO₂ compressor?

    Calculate the CO₂ compressor and compare ΔHig with H₂R − H₁R.

    Compare with an explanation

    The reference work is 2865 J/mol; the ideal gas gives 3430 J/mol, 20% more. At 30 bar the gas already has HR = −1.40 kJ/mol; at the outlet, 100 bar and about 400 K, the residual is more negative still. The outlet temperatures differ too: 399.7 K for the real gas and 387.7 K for the ideal gas, because the entropy balance carries SR at both ends. Attraction lowers the enthalpy of the compressed gas, so less work is needed than for an ideal gas between the same pressures.

    B. Why can an equation give Z well and HR badly?

    Calculate methane at 300 K and 100 bar. Compare the errors of PR in Z and in HR, and of SRK.

    Compare with an explanation

    Z depends on a(T) at one temperature; HR also depends on da/dT through d ln α/d ln Tr. PR gives Z of methane at 100 bar to 2.5% but HR = −1758 J/mol against −1604 (−10%), the same bias that makes its B too negative in Lab P2. SRK gives HR to 2%. The methane valve outlet follows: SRK 265.2 K, PR 262.9 K, reference 265.6 K.

    C. Why is GR small when HR and TSR are large?

    Calculate liquid CO₂ at 300 K and 100 bar and compare HR, TSR and GR.

    Compare with an explanation

    The reference gives HR = −10.85 kJ/mol and TSR = −9.07 kJ/mol, so GR = −1.78 kJ/mol. Condensing releases energy and orders the molecules; the two effects nearly cancel in G. That is why a liquid can be in equilibrium with its vapor at all: at the vapor pressure the residual Gibbs energies of the two phases are equal (Session P4). A small error in HR or SR is therefore a large relative error in GR.

    What these results can and cannot show

    • The reference H and S come from the same fundamental equation as Z, so integrating its Z reproduces its HR by construction. The test is of your numerics, and of the claim that PVT data suffice.
    • The valves are adiabatic and steady with negligible kinetic energy; the compressors are reversible and adiabatic. Real compressors need an efficiency, which changes the work but not the residual corrections.
    • Process errors combine the residual model and Cpig; B separates them.
