Lab P4 · Fugacity and the vapor pressure of a pure fluid

When do liquid and vapor coexist, and what is the fugacity of each?

Two phases of a pure fluid coexist when their fugacities are equal. The lab finds the vapor pressure of the van der Waals, SRK and Peng–Robinson equations from that condition, compares it with the NIST saturation tables of four fluids, and follows the fugacity along three isotherms through the phase change.

Data and method

The reference vapor pressure is interpolated in ln P between rows of the NIST saturation table (300 rows from the triple point or the table start to Tc). The cubic's vapor pressure is the pressure between its spinodals where ln φ of the liquid and vapor roots are equal, to about 10⁻¹² in ln φ. ΔvapH of the cubic is HR(vapor) − HR(liquid) at that pressure. The Poynting estimate uses the reference saturated liquid volume and φsat.

    A. Why does the fugacity bend at the vapor pressure?

    Choose the CO₂ isotherm at 280 K and the fugacity view. Compare the slope of f(P) below and above 41.6 bar.

    Compare with an explanation

    (∂ ln f/∂P)T = V/RT. In the vapor V ≈ 360–600 cm³/mol and f rises almost as fast as P; at 41.6 bar f = 31.1 bar in both phases. In the liquid V ≈ 50 cm³/mol, so f grows slowly: 35.1 bar at 100 bar and 42.7 bar at 200 bar. f is continuous at Psat because μ is; its slope jumps because V jumps.

    B. Why does van der Waals predict a vapor pressure that is far too high?

    Find the vapor pressure of propane at 300 K with the three cubics, and repeat at 200 K.

    Compare with an explanation

    Van der Waals has a constant a, so its attraction does not grow as T falls and the liquid is not stable enough: at 300 K it gives 17.4 bar against 9.98, and the error grows at lower T. SRK and PR make a grow as T falls through α(Tr, ω), with m(ω) fitted to vapor pressures, and are within about 1%.

    C. When can the Poynting factor be set to 1?

    Compare the Poynting factor for liquid water at 500 K and liquid CO₂ at 280 K, at 100 and 200 bar.

    Compare with an explanation

    The factor is exp[VL(P − Psat)/RT]. Water at 500 K: 1.039 at 100 bar and 1.095 at 200 bar. CO₂ at 280 K: 1.133 and 1.403, because its liquid volume is larger and RT smaller. Near 1 bar total pressure, as in most of Session 1, the factor is within 0.1% of 1. At 200 bar the constant-VL form overestimates the CO₂ fugacity by 2.3%: liquid CO₂ near its critical point is compressible.

    What these results can and cannot show

    • The reference ln φ and Psat come from one fundamental equation per fluid, so they satisfy equal fugacity exactly. They serve as data with uncertainties far below the model errors here.
    • SRK and PR were fitted to vapor pressures, so a good Psat tests the fit, not the physics. The enthalpy of vaporization and the liquid volume are independent tests.
    • The ice and supercooled-water pressures are a correlation of measurements (Murphy and Koop, 2005); below about 235 K supercooled water is not measured.
