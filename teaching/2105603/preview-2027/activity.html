Lab P6 · Excess Gibbs energy and activity coefficients

How far is a liquid mixture from ideal, and which model says so?

Measured VLE of 2-propanol (1) + water (2) at 60 and 80 kPa give activity coefficients through modified Raoult's law. The lab fits five GE models to the 60 kPa data, predicts both data sets, and reports infinite dilution, Henry's constants and the curvature that decides whether the liquid would split.

    4. Check your understanding

    Write first, then compare

    A. Why does γ₁ grow so much more at the dilute end than γ₂?

    Read γ∞ of both components for the two-parameter Margules fit.

    Compare with an explanation

    A 2-propanol molecule surrounded by water breaks hydrogen bonds between water molecules and cannot replace them fully; its escaping tendency is about 7–8 times the ideal-solution value. A water molecule in 2-propanol finds hydrogen-bonding partners, so γ₂∞ is only about 2–3.4. GE models capture this asymmetry with two parameters; the one-parameter Margules model forces γ₁∞ = γ₂∞.

    B. Why does the choice of objective change γ∞ so much?

    Fit NRTL with each objective and compare γ₁∞.

    Compare with an explanation

    GE/RT = x₁ ln γ₁ + x₂ ln γ₂ vanishes at both ends, so a GE fit is decided by the middle of the range and leaves the dilute ends free: γ₁∞ comes out 13–47 depending on the model. Fitting ln γ₁ and ln γ₂ gives the dilute points their weight: γ₁∞ ≈ 7.6–7.9 for every two-parameter model. What you fit decides what the model is good for.

    C. Can the fitted model predict a liquid split that does not occur?

    Read the minimum scaled curvature for each model and objective.

    Compare with an explanation

    2-propanol and water are fully miscible. With the GE objective, two-parameter Margules, van Laar and NRTL give a negative curvature near x₁ = 0.2–0.3: they predict a split. With the ln γ objective all curvatures stay positive. Wilson cannot predict a split for any parameters. Session 3 after the midterm treats stability in full.

    What these results can and cannot show

    • The stated composition uncertainty (0.02–0.03) is comparable with the dilute compositions themselves, so γ₁ below x₁ = 0.05 carries large uncertainty. Session 2 propagates it.
    • Constant parameters ignore the 18 K temperature span of each isobar; HE data would fix the temperature dependence.
    • A good fit at 60 kPa is a calibration; 80 kPa is the held-out test.
