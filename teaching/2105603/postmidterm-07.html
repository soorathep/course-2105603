One idea behind all six sessions

Equations to write without notes

Phases

\hat f_i^{\,V}=\hat f_i^{\,L}\;\Rightarrow\;y_i\Phi_iP=x_i\gamma_iP_i^{\rm sat}

\ln\Phi_i\approx\frac{B_{ii}(P-P_i^{\rm sat})}{RT}\quad\text{(virial, no Poynting)}

\sum_i\frac{z_i(K_i-1)}{1+\beta(K_i-1)}=0,\qquad K_i=\frac{\gamma_iP_i^{\rm sat}}{\Phi_iP}

\frac{\mathrm{TPD}(\mathbf w)}{RT}=\sum_iw_i\left[\ln w_i\gamma_i(\mathbf w)-\ln z_i\gamma_i(\mathbf z)\right]

Other equilibria

K=K_yK_\phi\left(\frac{P}{p^\circ}\right)^{\Delta\nu},\qquad \ln\phi_i\approx\frac{B_iP}{RT}

\ln\frac{K_2}{K_1}=-\frac{\Delta_rH^\circ}{R}\left(\frac1{T_2}-\frac1{T_1}\right)

E=E^\circ-\frac{RT}{nF}\ln Q,\qquad \Delta_rS=nF\left(\frac{\partial E}{\partial T}\right)_P

\sigma^2_{\ln\gamma_1}\approx\Big(\frac{u_x}{x_1}\Big)^2+\Big(\frac{u_y}{y_1}\Big)^2+\Big(\frac{u_P}{P}\Big)^2

For each equation, be ready to state what is assumed and which quantity is measured, supplied or calculated.

A1 · A flash when K depends on composition

The synthetic binary of Lab 01 at 350 K: P_1^{\rm sat}=150, P_2^{\rm sat}=60 kPa. The liquid follows one-parameter Margules with A=1; the vapor is ideal. Feed z_1=0.40, pressure 110 kPa.

  1. Ideal first. With K_i=P_i^{\rm sat}/P, solve Rachford–Rice. What does the root say about the phase state?
  2. Real liquid. Find the bubble and dew pressures of this feed with \gamma_i(x). Is 110 kPa between them?
  3. Iterate. Start from the ideal K, solve Rachford–Rice, update K_i=\gamma_i(x)P_i^{\rm sat}/P, repeat twice. Then find the converged x_1, y_1, \beta (for a binary, x_1 follows from the bubble-pressure equation at 110 kPa).
  4. Why does successive substitution converge slowly here?

A2 · What does one measured point really tell you?

At 350 K: x_1=1/3, y_1=5/9, P=90 kPa, P_1^{\rm sat}=150 kPa. Standard uncertainties u_x=u_y=0.005.

  1. Calculate \gamma_1 with an ideal vapor. Then include the vapor correction with B_{11}=-1000 cm^3 mol^{-1} (illustrative): \ln\Phi_1\approx B_{11}(P-P_1^{\rm sat})/RT. Which way does \gamma_1 move, and why does the sign follow from P<P_1^{\rm sat}?
  2. Propagate u_x and u_y into \sigma_{\ln\gamma_1}. Is the vapor correction larger or smaller than this uncertainty?
  3. If P_1^{\rm sat} came from an Antoine set 3 % too low, how large is the bias in \ln\gamma_1, and is it random or systematic?
  4. The data are isobaric. What extra term, and what extra measurement, does an area test need?

A3 · Is this liquid stable, and how do you prove it?

A binary follows one-parameter Margules with A=1050\ \mathrm K/T. Feed z_1=0.18 at 350 K, so A=3.

  1. Local test. Evaluate the scaled curvature 1-2Az_1z_2. What does its sign tell you, and what does it not?
  2. Global test. Evaluate the tangent-plane distance at the trial composition w_1=0.929. What does its sign prove?
  3. Equilibrium. In Lab 04 find both liquid compositions and the lever-rule split. Rebuild the feed.
  4. Temperature. Above which temperature is the liquid stable at every composition? What is that point called?

B1 · A vessel with two condensed phases

A closed 1.00 L vessel at 300 K contains 0.0086712 mol of A in total, pure solid A (sublimation pressure 10.0 kPa) and 10.0 g of a separate adsorbent with Langmuir uptake q=q_sbP_A/(1+bP_A), q_s=2.00 mol kg^{-1}, b=0.100 kPa^{-1}. Ideal gas, R=8.31446 L kPa mol^{-1} K^{-1}.

  1. Assume solid remains. Then P_A=P_{\rm sub}. How much A is needed in the gas and on the surface? Is the assumption consistent with the inventory?
  2. Assume no solid. Solve the inventory balance for P_A. Check that P_A<P_{\rm sub}.
  3. What is the smallest total inventory for which solid A survives? Use the phase rule to explain why adding more A then leaves P_A unchanged.

Exam-style problem · one measurement, one phase prediction

The same synthetic binary at 350 K (P_1^{\rm sat}=150, P_2^{\rm sat}=60 kPa) is measured at x_1=0.500: y_1=0.7143 and P=134.82 kPa.

  1. Infer \gamma_1 and \gamma_2. State your assumptions.
  2. Fit the one-parameter Margules model, \ln\gamma_1=Ax_2^2 and \ln\gamma_2=Ax_1^2. Do the two values of A agree? Why must they, for this model, at x_1=0.5?
  3. Predict whether this liquid can split into two phases.
  4. Locate any azeotrope: at an azeotrope \gamma_1P_1^{\rm sat}=\gamma_2P_2^{\rm sat}.
  5. Judge the prediction: what single additional measurement would test the model away from x_1=0.5?

Answer as in the exam: assumptions, equations, numbers with units, one check.

Exam-style problem · worked answer

Step Result
1 \gamma_1=\dfrac{0.7143(134.82)}{0.500(150)}=1.284, \gamma_2=\dfrac{0.2857(134.82)}{0.500(60)}=1.284. Ideal vapor, no Poynting, pure-liquid standard states.
2 A=\ln(1.284)/0.25=1.00 from both. The model is symmetric, so \gamma_1=\gamma_2 at x_1=0.5; a large mismatch would signal data or model error.
3 Splitting needs 1-2Ax_1x_2<0 somewhere, which requires A>2. With A=1.00 the liquid is stable at every composition.
4 \ln(\gamma_1/\gamma_2)=A(1-2x_1)=\ln(60/150)=-0.916 gives x_1=0.958 and P=150\exp[A(0.042)^2]=150.3 kPa: a maximum-pressure azeotrope.
5 Measure near the azeotrope or at a dilute end. One point at x_1=0.5 fixes A but cannot test the model’s shape.

Check. P=x_1\gamma_1P_1^{\rm sat}+x_2\gamma_2P_2^{\rm sat}=0.5(1.284)(150)+0.5(1.284)(60)=134.8 kPa, as measured.

How a complete answer is marked

A complete answer shows

  1. The equilibrium condition and the balance you used.
  2. Every assumption, stated before it is used.
  3. Numbers with units, or “dimensionless”.
  4. One independent check: rebuild the feed, close the inventory, recompute Q, or test a limit.
  5. One sentence on what the number means physically.

Common mistakes after the midterm

  • \beta outside 0–1 reported as an amount.
  • P^{\rm sat} at the wrong temperature, or Antoine outside its range.
  • Positive curvature taken as proof that a liquid will not split.
  • Gas moles found by subtraction, then never checked against the isotherm.
  • Pressure carrying units inside \ln Q; Q and K for different reactions as written.
  • A sign of E quoted without stating which electrode is the oxidation side.
