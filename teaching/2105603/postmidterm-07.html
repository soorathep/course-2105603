One idea behind all six sessions

Every problem after the midterm combined an equilibrium condition with a balance, then needed an independent check.

2 · Measured VLE y_iP=x_i\gamma_iP_i^{\rm sat} P^{\rm sat} from a stated correlation Units, log base, range of Antoine

Equations to write without notes

Phases

\mu_i^\alpha=\mu_i^\beta\iff \hat f_i^{\,\alpha}=\hat f_i^{\,\beta}

\gamma_i=\frac{y_iP}{x_iP_i^{\rm sat}}\quad\text{(ideal vapor, no Poynting)}

x_1\,\mathrm d\ln\gamma_1+x_2\,\mathrm d\ln\gamma_2=0\quad(T,P\ \text{fixed})

\frac{G^E}{RT}=Ax_1x_2\;\Rightarrow\;\ln\gamma_1=Ax_2^2

Other equilibria

q=\frac{q_sbP_A}{1+bP_A}

\Delta_rG=\Delta_rG^\circ+RT\ln Q

\ln K=-\frac{\Delta_rG^\circ}{RT}

\ln\frac{K_2}{K_1}=-\frac{\Delta_rH^\circ}{R}\left(\frac1{T_2}-\frac1{T_1}\right)

E=E^\circ-\frac{RT}{nF}\ln Q

For each equation, be ready to state what is assumed and which quantity is measured, supplied or calculated.

A1 · Which fraction of the feed becomes vapor?

At 350 K, an ideal binary mixture has P_1^{\rm sat}=150 kPa and P_2^{\rm sat}=60 kPa. The feed contains z_1=0.40 and the pressure is 90 kPa.

  1. Find x_1 and y_1 from P=x_1P_1^{\rm sat}+(1-x_1)P_2^{\rm sat} and y_1=x_1P_1^{\rm sat}/P.
  2. Find the vapor fraction \beta=(z_1-x_1)/(y_1-x_1) and rebuild the feed from both phases.
  3. Calculate the bubble and dew pressures of this feed. Is 90 kPa between them?

Going further. Repeat at 100 kPa with K_i=P_i^{\rm sat}/P and the Rachford–Rice equation \sum z_i(K_i-1)/[1+\beta(K_i-1)]=0. What does the root tell you?

Check in Lab 01 · VLE and flash

A2 · What if the compositions came from an experiment?

Suppose the measured values at 350 K are x_1=1/3, y_1=5/9 and P=90 kPa. Assume ideal vapor, negligible Poynting correction and a pure-liquid standard state.

  1. Use \gamma_1=y_1P/(x_1P_1^{\rm sat}) with P_1^{\rm sat}=150 kPa, then with 120 kPa. Which measured quantity changed?
  2. Before using an Antoine correlation, list the four things you must check.
  3. A model fits these data closely. What evidence across compositions would you need before calling the data thermodynamically consistent?

A3 · Which fraction forms the second liquid?

A different binary mixture follows the one-parameter Margules model with A=3. The overall mole fraction is z_1=0.18.

  1. In Lab 04 choose Margules · 1 parameter, enter A=3 and z_1=0.18, then Find equilibrium. Read both liquid compositions.
  2. Use the lever rule to find the fraction of the feed in the liquid richer in component 1. Rebuild the feed.
  3. Compare the common tangent with the homogeneous curve at z_1=0.18. Which state has the lower Gibbs energy?

Going further. The scaled curvature 1-2Ax_1x_2 is positive at z_1=0.18. Why does the liquid still split? Locate the spinodal.

B1 · Can the vessel reach 10 kPa?

A closed vessel contains 0.0086712 mol of A at 300 K. The free gas volume is 1.00 L. Treat the gas as ideal and use R=8.31446 L kPa mol^{-1} K^{-1}.

Vessel A. Pure solid A can sublime, with P_{\rm sub}=10.0 kPa at this temperature. How many moles are in the gas at that pressure? How many remain as solid?

Vessel B. The same inventory can occupy the gas or adsorb on 10.0 g of a separate adsorbent: q=q_sbP_A/(1+bP_A), q_s=2.00 mol kg^{-1}, b=0.100 kPa^{-1}. Test P_A=5.00 kPa. Does the inventory close?

Then predict, before calculating, how P_A changes when the adsorbent mass doubles to 20 g.

B2 · How far does the reaction go at a new temperature?

Use the Week 5 reaction \mathrm{A_2(g)}\rightleftharpoons 2\mathrm A(\mathrm g), starting from 1 mol of A_2, ideal gas, p^\circ=100 kPa. At 350 K, K=3.9532626 and \Delta_rH^\circ=50.0 kJ mol^{-1}, taken as constant.

  1. Calculate K at 400 K. Is the reaction endothermic or exothermic, and does the sign of the change agree?
  2. With f=\xi/(1\ \mathrm{mol}), K=\dfrac{4f^2}{1-f^2}\dfrac{P}{p^\circ}. Find f and y_A at 400 K and 100 kPa, then at 200 kPa.
  3. Recompute Q from your mole fractions and check the A-atom balance.

B3 · Which electrode would gain metal?

At 298.15 K, both electrodes use \mathrm{M^{2+}/M}. Take oxidation on the left, reduction on the right, and zero junction potential. Use a_L=0.040 and a_R=0.40.

  1. Write the reaction with each ion in its own compartment. Which activity goes in the numerator of Q?
  2. With E^\circ=0, what is K? Compare Q with K and predict which electrode gains metal.
  3. Calculate E=E^\circ-(0.05915935\ {\rm V}/n)\log_{10}Q and \Delta_rG=-nFE with n=2, F=96485 C mol^{-1}. Then exchange the two activities. What changes?

Going further. Repeat at 323.15 K. Which part of the Nernst equation depends on temperature, and does the direction change?

Exam-style problem · one measurement, one phase prediction

The same synthetic binary at 350 K (P_1^{\rm sat}=150, P_2^{\rm sat}=60 kPa) is measured at x_1=0.500: y_1=0.7143 and P=134.82 kPa.

  1. Infer \gamma_1 and \gamma_2. State your assumptions.
  2. Fit the one-parameter Margules model, \ln\gamma_1=Ax_2^2 and \ln\gamma_2=Ax_1^2. Do the two values of A agree? Why must they, for this model, at x_1=0.5?
  3. Predict whether this liquid can split into two phases.
  4. Locate any azeotrope: at an azeotrope \gamma_1P_1^{\rm sat}=\gamma_2P_2^{\rm sat}.
  5. Judge the prediction: what single additional measurement would test the model away from x_1=0.5?

Answer as in the exam: assumptions, equations, numbers with units, one check.

Exam-style problem · worked answer

Step Result
1 \gamma_1=\dfrac{0.7143(134.82)}{0.500(150)}=1.284, \gamma_2=\dfrac{0.2857(134.82)}{0.500(60)}=1.284. Ideal vapor, no Poynting, pure-liquid standard states.
2 A=\ln(1.284)/0.25=1.00 from both. The model is symmetric, so \gamma_1=\gamma_2 at x_1=0.5; a large mismatch would signal data or model error.
3 Splitting needs 1-2Ax_1x_2<0 somewhere, which requires A>2. With A=1.00 the liquid is stable at every composition.
4 \ln(\gamma_1/\gamma_2)=A(1-2x_1)=\ln(60/150)=-0.916 gives x_1=0.958 and P=150\exp[A(0.042)^2]=150.3 kPa: a maximum-pressure azeotrope.
5 Measure near the azeotrope or at a dilute end. One point at x_1=0.5 fixes A but cannot test the model’s shape.

Check. P=x_1\gamma_1P_1^{\rm sat}+x_2\gamma_2P_2^{\rm sat}=0.5(1.284)(150)+0.5(1.284)(60)=134.8 kPa, as measured.

How a complete answer is marked

A complete answer shows

  1. The equilibrium condition and the balance you used.
  2. Every assumption, stated before it is used.
  3. Numbers with units, or “dimensionless”.
  4. One independent check: rebuild the feed, close the inventory, recompute Q, or test a limit.
  5. One sentence on what the number means physically.

Common mistakes after the midterm

  • \beta outside 0–1 reported as an amount.
  • P^{\rm sat} at the wrong temperature, or Antoine outside its range.
  • Positive curvature taken as proof that a liquid will not split.
  • Gas moles found by subtraction, then never checked against the isotherm.
  • Pressure carrying units inside \ln Q; Q and K for different reactions as written.
  • A sign of E quoted without stating which electrode is the oxidation side.
