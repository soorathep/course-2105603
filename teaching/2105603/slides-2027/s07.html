One condition, six forms

Session Working equation What the model supplies
1 · VLE, flash yiΦiP = xiγiPisat; Σzi(Ki − 1)/[1 + β(Ki − 1)] = 0 γ, Psat
2 · measured VLE γi = yiΦiP/(xiPisat) GE model and its weights
3 · LLE xiαγiα = xiβγiβ; TPD(w) ≥ 0 τ(T)
4 · SLE, adsorption ln(x₁γ₁) = −(ΔfusH/R)(1/T − 1/Tm); n = PV/RT + mq(P) fusion data, w; isotherm
5 · reactions Π(yiφiP/P°)νi = K(T) H(T), S(T), Kφ
6 · cells E = −ΔrG/nF; aKCl = (mγ±)² γ±(m), its T dependence

Every row is μi equal across phases, or Σνiμi = 0. What changes is the reference state and the model.

The evidence ladder

Weakest to strongest

Rung Course example
1 · calibration one tie line, exactly
2 · consistency O/C ratio closes; TPD ≥ 0 at both liquids
3 · held-out fit 60 kPa, check 80 kPa: 0.164
4 · independent property LLE data rule out a VLE-fitted split
5 · different method calorimetry for ∂ln γ±/∂T

A claim is only as strong as the highest rung it reached.

Extrapolation reaches none: the a + b/T model’s gap closing at 324 K lies beyond the last tie line at 316 K.

Some claims cannot be reached with the data in hand: the sign of w for naphthalene–benzene, or dγ±/dT from KCl data with U = 0.03.

Saying so is part of the answer.

Exam-style problem

A colleague fits ln γ₁ = (w/RT)x₂² to naphthalene solubility in benzene and reports “w = −522 J/mol: benzene is a better-than-ideal solvent”. (a) Reproduce the claim from the data in Lab 10 E. (b) Is the claim supported? (c) What would you report instead, and what one measurement would settle the sign?

25 minutes · alone

Answer in this order: equation, assumptions with their size, numbers, check, conclusion and its limit.

You may use the labs. State every number you take from them.

Exam-style problem: a model answer

(a) ln(x₁γ₁) = −(ΔfusH/R)(1/T − 1/Tm), ΔCp neglected. With the article’s DSC data (355.04 K, 20.87 kJ/mol) and all ten rows, least squares in ln x₁: w = −522 J/mol, RMSE 0.008.

(b) No. With the NIST data (353.39 K, 19.0 kJ/mol), w = +238 J/mol. The two fusion sets differ by about their stated uncertainty, and the sign of w follows them. x₁ at 298.15 K is 0.289 or 0.287 either way.

(c) Report: near-ideal, |ln γ₁| ≤ 0.13 between 286 and 326 K with either fusion data set; x₁(298.15 K) = 0.288 ± 0.001 for either fusion data set. Do not report the sign of w.

To settle the sign: a measurement that does not pass through the solid, such as HE or VLE of liquid naphthalene + benzene, or ΔfusH measured to better than ±0.5 kJ/mol.

The model answer reaches rung 1 for w and rung 3 for the prediction.

How answers are marked

Equation and reference state (25 %). The right equilibrium condition, with standard states and units stated.

Assumptions with their size (20 %). Not “assume ideal”, but “Φ = 1 changes ln γ by 0.011”.

Numbers that can be checked (25 %). Intermediate values, units, and a balance or limit that closes.

Conclusion and its limit (30 %). What the evidence supports, which rung it reached, what would raise it.

A correct number with no limit stated earns less than a well-argued answer with an arithmetic slip.

Summary

One condition Equal chemical potential, in six forms. What changes is the reference state and the model.

Evidence Calibration, consistency, held-out, independent property, different method. A claim is only as strong as its highest rung.

Code Converging is not checking. Recompute one quantity independently, close a balance, test a limit.

Session question: the strongest evidence in this course was held-out data and independent properties: tie lines the fit did not use, LLE against a VLE fit, a second fusion data set. Where we had neither, the honest answer was to say what the data cannot decide.
