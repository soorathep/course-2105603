Choosing a property model

Situation Start with Check first
Gas or supercritical fluid, nonpolar, low density virial, B(T) ρ below about half of ρc
Pure fluid with a reference equation of state the reference equation (NIST) its stated uncertainty in this region
Hydrocarbons and light gases, high pressure, both phases cubic EOS (PR, SRK) with kij Z and Psat against data; liquid volume
Polar or associating liquids, low pressure γ–φ: Wilson, NRTL, UNIQUAC; a Psat correlation VLE in range, γ∞, stability
A liquid split is possible NRTL or UNIQUAC, not Wilson LLE data, not a VLE fit alone
Dilute solute, or a gas above its Tc in a liquid Henry’s law, γi* a measured Hi or γ∞
No mixture data UNIFAC or a predictive EOS treat it as a first estimate; measure before design

A table like this is a starting point (Carlson, 1996). The evidence for the case in hand decides.

The evidence ladder

Weakest to strongest

Rung Example before the midterm
1 · calibration B fitted up to 1 mol/L; NRTL fitted to GE
2 · consistency Gibbs–Duhem on measured γ; a balance that closes
3 · held-out fit at 60 kPa, predict 80 kPa: RMS y₁ 0.046
4 · independent property PR built on Psat, checked on VL: +23% for water at 500 K
5 · different method B12 measured, against a combining rule

A claim is only as strong as the highest rung it reached.

Extrapolation reaches none: γ₁∞ lies beyond the most dilute point, x₁ = 0.0063.

Some claims cannot be decided with the data in hand: γ₁∞ of 2-propanol in water to better than tens of percent, when u(x) = 0.02.

Saying so is part of the answer. Session 7 after the midterm uses the same ladder.

Exam-style problem

A design note sizes a 1 m³ CO₂ buffer vessel at 280 K and 100 bar, and a compressor from 30 to 100 bar at 300 K inlet, with the ideal gas and “a 10% margin”. (a) Quantify the error in the stored mass, the compressor work and the outlet temperature. (b) Is the margin adequate? (c) What would you use instead, and what evidence supports it?

25 minutes · alone

Answer in this order: equation, assumptions with their size, numbers, check, conclusion and its limit.

You may use the labs. State every number you take from them.

Exam-style problem: a model answer

(a) Mass m = PVM/(ZRT). The vessel holds compressed liquid: the reference gives 938 kg, the ideal gas 189 kg (−80%). Isentropic work W = ΔH with Sout = Sin, H = Hig + HR: reference 2865 J/mol and 399.7 K; ideal gas 3430 J/mol (+20%) and 387.7 K (12 K low).

(b) No. The mass is low by a factor of 5, the unsafe direction for an inventory or release estimate. The work error is twice the margin and errs on the safe side; the outlet temperature errs on the unsafe side.

(c) For pure CO₂, use the reference equation of state (Span and Wagner), from which the NIST values come. If a cubic is needed, PR gives 939 kg, 2851 J/mol and 403.4 K. That agreement is a held-out check (rung 3) at two states: PR uses only Tc, Pc and ω. Near the critical point (Tr = 1.05) its dense-phase Z error rises to 5.3%, so check it again if the conditions move.

A margin covers known, bounded uncertainty. It does not cover using a model outside the region where it holds.

How answers are marked

Equation and reference state (25 %). The right relation, with the reference state, units and path stated.

Assumptions with their size (20 %). Not “assume an ideal vapor”, but “φ̂ = 1 changes p₁ by about 1%”.

Numbers that can be checked (25 %). Intermediate values, units, and a balance or limit that closes.

Conclusion and its limit (30 %). What the evidence supports, which rung it reached, what would raise it.

A correct number with no limit stated earns less than a well-argued answer with an arithmetic slip.

Summary

One chain PVT and Cpig give Z, residual properties, fugacity; mixtures add composition, liquids add γ.

Choosing Start from the fluid, the phase and the pressure. Then check the property the result depends on, in the region of use.

Evidence Calibration, consistency, held-out, independent property, different method. A claim is only as strong as its highest rung.

Session question: choose by fluid, phase and pressure, then test the model on a property it was not built on. The strongest evidence before the midterm was of that kind: the liquid volume against a Psat-fitted EOS, 80 kPa VLE against a 60 kPa fit, a measured B12 against a combining rule.
