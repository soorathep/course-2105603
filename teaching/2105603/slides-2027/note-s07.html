3. The evidence ladder

From weakest to strongest:

  1. Calibration: the model reproduces the data it was fitted to. Two equations, two parameters: always possible.
  2. Internal consistency: Gibbs–Duhem, element balances, TPD ≥ 0 at the fitted phases.
  3. Held-out data: same kind of measurement, conditions the fit did not use.
  4. Independent property: a different quantity the model also predicts, such as LLE from a VLE fit, or HE from τ(T).
  5. Different method: calorimetry against EMF, DSC against solubility.

A claim is only as strong as the highest rung it reached. Extrapolation beyond the data, such as the 324 K closing of the cyclohexane–methanol gap, reaches none.

4. Checking code you did not write line by line

From Session 2 you have used AI assistants to write code. The checks that caught real errors in this course were:

  • a quantity recomputed independently (Psat, ln K, an activity);
  • a balance that must close (atoms, moles, O/C ratio);
  • a limit the answer must satisfy (0 < β < 1, xα ≠ xβ, TPD at w = z equals 0);
  • the objective re-evaluated at the reported parameters;
  • a known case reproduced (the Lab E reference values).

Convergence and a small residual check the solver, not the thermodynamics.
