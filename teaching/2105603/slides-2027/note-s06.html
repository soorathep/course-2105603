Electrochemical equilibrium: a voltage from activities

Pre-class note for Session 6 · about 20 minutes

Soorathep Kheawhom · 2105603 Advanced Chemical Engineering Thermodynamics

Draft for 2027. If you are taking the course this year, use the current course page.

Why read this. Session 5 asked how far a reaction goes. Session 6 holds a reaction at equilibrium with an external voltage and asks what that voltage is. The cell EMF measures ΔrG directly, so it is also one of the most precise ways to measure activity coefficients. Work the three questions at the end and bring one question of your own.

1. EMF and Gibbs energy

For a reversible cell transferring n electrons per reaction,

\Delta_rG=-nFE,\qquad E=E^\circ-\frac{RT}{nF}\ln\prod_i a_i^{\nu_i}.

The Nernst equation is the reaction-equilibrium condition of Session 5 with an electrical work term added. E° is fixed by the standard states; for solutes we use molality with m° = 1 mol/kg.

2. Mean ionic activity

A single-ion activity cannot be measured: every measurable quantity involves a neutral combination of ions. For a 1:1 salt such as KCl,

a_{\text{KCl}}=a_{\text{K}^+}a_{\text{Cl}^-}=(m\gamma_\pm)^2.

The square is what makes a KCl cell respond with 2RT/F per unit of ln(mγ±), not RT/F.

3. Debye–Hückel and its range

At low ionic strength I = ½Σmizi², long-range Coulomb interactions give the limiting law

\log_{10}\gamma_\pm=-A|z_+z_-|\sqrt I,\qquad A=0.509\ (\text{kg/mol})^{1/2}\text{ at }298\ \text{K}.

A is on the log₁₀ basis; on the ln basis it is 1.172. The limiting law is a law of the dilute limit, not a model for 1 mol/kg. An extended form adds an ion-size term and a linear term:

\ln\gamma_\pm=-\frac{A_{\ln}\sqrt m}{1+B\sqrt m}+C\,m.

Measured data (KCl, 298.2 K). Nasiri-Lohehsara and Ghalami-Choobar (2018) report γ± from 0.012 to 2.25 mol/kg, with an expanded uncertainty of about 0.03. At 1.0 mol/kg they give 0.597; the limiting law gives 0.310. A fit of the extended form to the twelve rows gives b = 1.135 (kg/mol)1/2 and c = 0.042 kg/mol, with an RMSE of 0.022 in ln γ±.

4. A concentration cell

Two cells Ag|AgCl|KCl(m)|K⁺-ISE at m₁ and m₂, connected in opposition, cancel E° and leave

E=\frac{2RT}{F}\ln\frac{m_2\gamma_{\pm,2}}{m_1\gamma_{\pm,1}}.

With m₁ = 0.01 and m₂ = 1.0 mol/kg at 298.15 K, the ideal value is 0.2366 V. With the fitted γ± (0.901 and 0.603) it is 0.2160 V, 21 mV lower. The uncertainty in γ± alone, ±0.03 on each side, carries about ±3 mV into E.

A cell with transference, where the two solutions meet at a liquid junction, has an extra junction potential that depends on ion mobilities. Lab 13 neglects it; Session 6 says when that is acceptable.

5. Temperature: entropy and heat

From ΔrG = −nFE,

\Delta_rS=nF\Big(\frac{\partial E}{\partial T}\Big)_P,\qquad \Delta_rH=-nF\Big(E-T\frac{\partial E}{\partial T}\Big).

For an ideal concentration cell E is proportional to T, so ΔrH = 0: all the electrical work comes from TΔS, heat drawn from the surroundings. With real γ±, ∂ ln γ±/∂T adds the partial molar enthalpies. The KCl data at 298.2 and 308.2 K give γ± at 1 mol/kg of 0.603 and 0.606: a difference of 0.004, far inside the 0.03 uncertainty. These data cannot resolve that derivative; calorimetry can.

6. Before Session 6

Write short answers. We use them in the first 30 minutes.

  1. Factor 2. Show from aKCl = (mγ±)² that the KCl cell pair gives 2RT/F, not RT/F, and compute the ideal EMF for 0.01 and 1.0 mol/kg at 298.15 K.
  2. Range. At 0.0124 mol/kg the data give γ± = 0.927 ± 0.027 and the limiting law 0.878. Is the difference larger than the stated uncertainty? Which would you question first, the lowest row or the law, and why?
  3. Heat. An ideal concentration cell delivers 0.2366 V at 298.15 K. How much heat per mole of transferred KCl does it absorb when discharged reversibly, and from where?
