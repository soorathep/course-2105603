Excess properties and activity coefficients

Pre-class note for Session P6 · about 20 minutes

Soorathep Kheawhom, Ph.D., FRSC · 2105603 Advanced Chemical Engineering Thermodynamics

Draft for 2027. If you are taking the course this year, use the current course page.

Why read this. Session P5 defined the ideal solution, f̂i = xifi. Liquid mixtures depart from it, often by a factor of several at high dilution. This note defines the excess properties and the activity coefficient that measure the departure, the standard states the activity coefficient refers to, and the models used to represent it. Work the three questions at the end and bring one question of your own.

1. Excess properties

An excess property is the difference between the real mixture and an ideal solution at the same T, P and composition:

M^E=M-M^{\text{id}},\qquad G^E=H^E-TS^E.

For V and H the ideal-solution value is ΣxiMi*, so VE = ΔVmix and HE = ΔHmix. The Gibbs energy of mixing is the ideal term plus GE:

\frac{\Delta g_{\text{mix}}}{RT}=\sum_ix_i\ln x_i+\frac{G^E}{RT}.

GE > 0 (positive deviation) means each component prefers its own kind; GE < 0 means unlike pairs attract. The temperature dependence is set by the excess enthalpy, ∂(GE/RT)/∂T = −HE/RT². A model with constant parameters therefore assumes HE ≈ 0 over the range of use.

2. The activity coefficient

The activity coefficient compares the fugacity of a component with its ideal-solution value:

\gamma_i=\frac{\hat f_i}{x_if_i^\circ},\qquad \ln\gamma_i=\Big(\frac{\partial(nG^E/RT)}{\partial n_i}\Big)_{T,P,n_j}.

ln γi is the partial molar property of GE/RT, so by Session P5

\frac{G^E}{RT}=\sum_ix_i\ln\gamma_i,\qquad\sum_ix_i\,d\ln\gamma_i=0\quad(T,P\ \text{fixed}).

With the pure liquid as the standard state, f_i^\circ=\varphi_i^{\text{sat}}P_i^{\text{sat}}\mathcal{P}_i (Session P4). Equal fugacity with the vapor, \hat f_i^{V}=y_i\hat\varphi_i^{V}P, then gives the γ–φ form used in Sessions 1 and 2 after the midterm:

y_i\Phi_iP=x_i\gamma_iP_i^{\text{sat}}(T),\qquad\Phi_i=\frac{\hat\varphi_i^{V}}{\varphi_i^{\text{sat}}\mathcal{P}_i}.

At low pressure Φi ≈ 1 (here within about 1%), and the equation becomes modified Raoult’s law, yiP = xiγiPisat.

Worked example. 2-propanol (1) + water (2) at 60 kPa (Barbieri et al., 2024): x₁ = 0.1401, y₁ = 0.4769, T = 342.92 K. The Antoine equations give P₁sat = 60.12 and P₂sat = 30.87 kPa. Then

γ₁ = 0.4769 × 60/(0.1401 × 60.12) = 3.397, γ₂ = 0.5231 × 60/(0.8599 × 30.87) = 1.182,

GE/RT = 0.1401 ln 3.397 + 0.8599 ln 1.182 = 0.315.

3. Standard states

The reference fugacity fi° is a choice; f̂i is not. The activity ai = f̂i/fi° = xiγi depends on that choice; Sessions 5 and 6 after the midterm work with activities (for a gas, ai = yiφ̂iP/P°). Two conventions are common for liquids.

  • Raoult (symmetric). fi° is the fugacity of pure liquid i at T and P, and γi → 1 as xi → 1. Natural for components that exist as liquids at T over the whole range.
  • Henry (unsymmetric). f̂i → xiHi as xi → 0, with Henry’s constant Hi = γi∞fi ≈ γi∞Pisat. The activity coefficient γi* = γi/γi∞ tends to one at infinite dilution. Natural for a dilute solute, and necessary for a supercritical gas, which has no pure liquid at T.

A third convention, on the molality scale, is used for electrolytes in Session 6 after the midterm: ai = miγi/m° with m° = 1 mol/kg, and γi → 1 as m → 0. Whatever the convention, γifi° is the same, so mixing a γ from one convention with an fi° from another is an error of a factor γi∞.

4. Models for GE

A model is an expression for GE/RT(x); the ln γi follow by differentiation, so they satisfy Gibbs–Duhem by construction.

  • Margules. GE/RT = x₁x₂(A₂₁x₁ + A₁₂x₂), so ln γ₁∞ = A₁₂ and ln γ₂∞ = A₂₁. With A₁₂ = A₂₁ = A it has one parameter.
  • van Laar. ln γ₁ = A₁₂(1 + A₁₂x₁/A₂₁x₂)−2, also with ln γi∞ = Aij.
  • Wilson. GE/RT = −Σxi ln(ΣxjΛij), with Λij > 0. Good for miscible polar mixtures. It cannot predict a liquid–liquid split for any parameters.
  • NRTL. A local-composition model with τ₁₂, τ₂₁ and a non-randomness parameter α, usually fixed between 0.2 and 0.47. It can represent both VLE and LLE.
  • UNIQUAC and UNIFAC. A combinatorial term for size and shape plus a residual term; UNIFAC builds the parameters from functional groups and is used when no mixture data exist.

One point is not enough. Fitting two-parameter Margules to the single point of Section 2 alone gives A₁₂ = 3.58 and A₂₁ = −3.30, that is γ₂∞ = 0.04, which contradicts the positive deviation measured everywhere else. A fit to all 18 points at 60 kPa gives A₁₂ = 2.02 and A₂₁ = 0.78 (γ∞ = 7.6 and 2.2). The parameters are only as good as the range and accuracy of the data behind them.

5. Stability of one liquid phase

A binary liquid is locally stable when Δgmix/RT is convex in x₁:

1+x_1x_2\frac{\partial^2(G^E/RT)}{\partial x_1^2}>0.

A fitted model that violates this inside the composition range predicts two liquids. That is a property of the model and the fit, and it should be compared with what the mixture does. Session 3 after the midterm develops stability and liquid–liquid equilibrium in full.

6. Before Session P6

Write short answers. We use them in the first 30 minutes.

  1. Infinite dilution. Show from the Margules expression for GE/RT that ln γ₁ = x₂²[A₁₂ + 2(A₂₁ − A₁₂)x₁], and hence that ln γ₁∞ = A₁₂.
  2. Henry’s constant. With γ₁∞ = 7.56 and P₁sat(345 K) = 65.8 kPa, compute H₁ for 2-propanol in water. Estimate the partial pressure of 2-propanol above a 1 mol% solution at 345 K, and compare it with the ideal-solution value.
  3. Stability. For one-parameter Margules, GE/RT = Ax₁x₂, show that the criterion of Section 5 becomes 1 − 2Ax₁x₂ > 0, so a split first appears at A = 2. Fitting A to the single point of Section 2 gives A = 2.62. What does that predict, and why should you doubt it?
